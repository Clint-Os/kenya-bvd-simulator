1 · Will two imported cases take off?

Ebola transmission is overdispersed: a few cases drive most spread, so many introductions die out by chance. This is a branching-process estimate from two index cases; it updates live with the reproduction number and the dispersion k.

Lower k = more superspreading and more early extinction. Ebola ≈ 0.18 (Althaus 2015).

2 · If it takes off, how many isolation beds does Mombasa need?

Stochastic results (two index cases, R₀ 2.0, 60% community-contact reduction). Beds are a hard cap: a case that cannot be admitted keeps transmitting. Port Reitz has 4 beds; the rest would sit at a Mombasa county isolation unit such as Utange.

How the model works

Each city is split into formal and informal-settlement populations moving through Susceptible → Exposed → Infectious in the community → Hospitalised in isolation → dead but not yet buried (Funeral) → Removed, after Legrand et al. (2007). Transmission happens in the community, in hospital and at unsafe burials. Admission to isolation is capped by beds: a case that cannot be admitted keeps transmitting, which is why the reproduction number more than doubles once wards are full. Two cases are seeded in each city's informal settlements; the response starts a set delay after the first isolation admission.
